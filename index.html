Built for the farms the big firms skip

Most U.S. cattle operations are small: 55% of farms with beef cows run fewer than 20 head (2022 USDA Census of Agriculture). The usual price-protection tools were built for much bigger operations. One CME Feeder Cattle futures contract is 50,000 lbs, about a semi-load of cattle, and brokers often want a $5,000 to $10,000 account just to start.

USDA already offers a better fit: Livestock Risk Protection (LRP), subsidized insurance that pays if cattle prices fall below a price you lock in. It covers as few as one head. But research puts adoption around 7%, and "choice overload" across coverage levels and plan lengths is a big reason why.

Sim Hedge makes that choice simple, using your own numbers, so you can see a full breakdown of each coverage plan.
