2 Your lots

A lot is a group of similar cattle sold together. Make a separate lot whenever the type (feeder steers, feeder heifers, or fat cattle), weight class (feeder: under 600 lbs or 600 to 1,000 lbs), or sale date is different. Each lot gets its own LRP plan.

3 How LRP pays on a lot

LRP pays on one national price at the end of your coverage: the CME Feeder Cattle Index for feeder cattle, or the CME Live Cattle Index for fat cattle. What your cattle actually sell for doesn't change the payout. Example: a lot of 18 steers at 550 lbs is 99 cwt insured, with a $308 floor:

Index ends atLRP paysWhy
$300$792($308 − $300) × 99 cwt
$295$1,287($308 − $295) × 99 cwt
$310$0Index above the floor, even if your local sale was only $300

That last row is basis risk: your sale barn and the national index don't always move together.

4 After your LRP expires

You don't have to sell when coverage ends.

  • Feeder cattle: sell them or keep them. You can keep growing them after coverage ends. LRP pays on the index on the end date either way, as long as you owned the cattle during the last 60 days of coverage.
  • Fat cattle: plan to sell them. They're already finished, so unlike feeder cattle you can't keep growing them into a heavier class. Holding them longer just adds feed cost.

Keep proof you owned them

If LRP pays, the insurance company can ask you to prove you owned the cattle during the last 60 days. Records that usually work:

  • Sale barn settlement sheets or sale receipts
  • Bills of sale or purchase receipts
  • Brand inspection certificates (in states that brand-inspect)
  • Vet, vaccination, or health records in your name
  • Ear tag or RFID records
  • Feedlot or custom-feeding invoices, for cattle on feed

Ask your agent which records they accept.

Set up ownership with your agent before you buy

The name on your LRP application has to match who owns the cattle: you, your spouse, a partnership, or an LLC. If someone else owns a share, that has to be listed too. Don't lose a payment because the application wasn't filled out right or ownership records are missing.

If the index ended below your floor

File a claim (a Notice of Probable Loss) with your agent within 60 days after coverage ends. The insurance company usually pays within about 30 days.

The premium isn't due until the end

You don't pay for LRP when you buy it. The premium is billed after coverage ends, so that money stays in your account, or off your operating loan, the whole time. Example: a $1,700 premium billed 21 weeks after you buy, at an 8% operating-loan rate, is about $55 of interest you don't pay.

Confirm claim deadlines, ownership rules, and billing with your agent. Rules can change each year.
